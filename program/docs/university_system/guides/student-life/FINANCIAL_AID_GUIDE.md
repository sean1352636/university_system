## FAFSA & Application

### Filing the FAFSA

**Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA):**
- Required for all federal and most institutional aid
- Opens October 1 for following academic year
- Priority deadline: **March 1** (for fall enrollment)

**How to File:**
1. Visit `studentaid.gov`
2. Create FSA ID (if you don't have one)
3. Complete FAFSA online:
   - Your demographic information
   - Parent information (if dependent)
   - Financial information (tax returns from 2 years prior)
   - School codes (add our university)
4. Submit FAFSA
5. Receive Student Aid Report (SAR) via email
6. Check for errors and make corrections if needed

**After Filing:**
1. University receives FAFSA data (typically 3-5 days)
2. Financial Aid Office reviews application
3. May request additional documentation (verification)
4. Award letter generated (typically 2-4 weeks)

### Verification Process

**What is Verification?**
- Random selection by Dept. of Education (about 30% of applicants)
- Required to verify FAFSA information accuracy
- Must complete before aid disbursed

**Documents Typically Requested:**
- **Tax Return Transcript** (IRS form, not tax return itself)
- **W-2s** (yours and parents' if dependent)
- **Verification Worksheet** (provided by financial aid office)
- **Citizenship documents** (if flagged)

**Submitting Verification Documents:**
1. Navigate to **Financial Aid** → **Requirements**
2. View list of required documents
3. Upload documents (PDF or JPG)
4. Submit for review
5. Receive confirmation email
6. Processing: 2-3 weeks

**IRS Data Retrieval Tool:**
- Use this tool within FAFSA to auto-import tax data
- Fastest and most accurate method
- Available at `studentaid.gov`

### Dependency Status

**Dependent Student:**
- Under 24 years old
- Not married, no dependents
- Must report parent information on FAFSA

**Independent Student:**
- Age 24 or older
- Married
- Have dependent children
- Veteran or active duty military
- Orphan or ward of court
- Graduate student

**Dependency Override:**
- In rare cases, can request override (abuse, abandonment)
- Contact financial aid office
- Requires documentation and counselor verification

## Understanding Your Award

### Award Letter

**Receiving Your Award Letter:**
1. Navigate to **Financial Aid** → **Award Letter**
2. View for each academic year
3. Breakdown by:
   - Fall semester
   - Spring semester
   - Summer (if applicable)

**Award Letter Components:**

| Category | Description | Repayment Required? |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Grants** | Need-based gift aid | No |
| **Scholarships** | Merit or achievement-based aid | No |
| **Work-Study** | Part-time job earnings | No (you earn it) |
| **Federal Loans** | Borrowed money | Yes |
| **Parent PLUS Loan** | Parent-borrowed funds | Yes (parent repays) |

**Example Award Letter:**
```
Total Cost of Attendance: $45,000
  Tuition & Fees: $35,000
  Room & Board: $8,000
  Books & Supplies: $1,200
  Personal Expenses: $800

Financial Aid Award:
  Pell Grant: $6,500
  University Grant: $15,000
  State Grant: $2,000
  Merit Scholarship: $5,000
  Work-Study: $2,500
  Federal Direct Loan: $5,500
  ---
  Total Aid: $36,500

Expected Family Contribution (EFC): $8,500
Remaining Need (Gap): $0
```

### Accepting Your Award

**Accept, Reduce, or Decline Aid:**
1. Navigate to **Award Letter** → **Accept/Decline**
2. Review each award
3. Choose action:
   - **Accept**: I want this aid
   - **Reduce**: I want less (common with loans)
   - **Decline**: I don't want this aid
4. Loans require additional steps:
   - Complete Entrance Counseling (first-time borrowers)
   - Sign Master Promissory Note (MPN)
5. Submit acceptance
6. Receive confirmation email

**Deadlines:**
- Accept awards by **May 1** for fall enrollment
- Late acceptance may reduce aid availability

### Cost of Attendance (COA)

**What's Included:**
- **Direct Costs** (billed by university):
  - Tuition and fees
  - Room and board (if on-campus)
- **Indirect Costs** (you pay separately):
  - Books and supplies
  - Transportation
  - Personal expenses

**COA Adjustments:**
- Living off-campus: Different room/board allowance
- Part-time enrollment: Reduced COA
- Dependent care: Can request increase
- Disability expenses: Can request increase
- Study abroad: Different COA

## Scholarships

### Types of Scholarships

**Merit-Based:**
- Academic achievement (GPA, test scores)
- Talent (music, art, athletics)
- Leadership and service

**Need-Based:**
- Demonstrated financial need
- May combine with merit criteria

**Department/Major-Specific:**
- Engineering, nursing, education, etc.
- Apply through department

**Identity-Based:**
- First-generation students
- Underrepresented minorities
- Gender-specific
- Geographic (local, state)

### Finding Scholarships

**University Scholarship Search:**
1. Navigate to **Financial Aid** → **Scholarship Search**
2. Complete profile (one-time):
   - Academic info (GPA, major, test scores)
   - Demographics
   - Activities and interests
3. View matched scholarships:
   - Award amount
   - Eligibility criteria
   - Application deadline
   - Renewal requirements
4. Click **Apply** for each scholarship

**External Scholarship Search:**
- `scholarships.com`
- `fastweb.com`
- Local organizations (Rotary, Kiwanis, etc.)
- Employer scholarships (parent's employer)

### Applying for Scholarships

**Typical Application Requirements:**
- **Personal Statement/Essay**: 500-1000 words on prompt
- **Letters of Recommendation**: 1-3 from teachers, counselors, employers
- **Transcript**: Official or unofficial
- **Resume**: Academic and extracurricular activities
- **FAFSA**: For need-based scholarships

**Application Tips:**
1. **Start Early**: Don't wait until deadline
2. **Tailor Essays**: Customize for each scholarship
3. **Proofread**: Spelling and grammar matter
4. **Meet Deadlines**: Late applications not accepted
5. **Request Letters Early**: Give recommenders 2-3 weeks notice

### Scholarship Renewal

**Maintaining Scholarships:**
- Most scholarships require renewal each year
- Common requirements:
  - **Minimum GPA**: Often 3.0 or 3.5
  - **Full-Time Enrollment**: 12+ credits per semester
  - **Satisfactory Progress**: On track to graduate
  - **Major**: Some require staying in specific major

**Renewal Process:**
1. Check renewal criteria in scholarship details
2. Meet requirements each academic year
3. Automatic renewal (for most scholarships)
4. Notification if scholarship not renewed (failure to meet criteria)

**If You Lose Scholarship:**
- Review why (GPA, enrollment, etc.)
- May appeal if extenuating circumstances
- Plan to replace lost aid (loans, work, etc.)

## Grants

### Federal Grants

**Pell Grant:**
- Maximum: ~$7,395 per year (varies annually)
- Based on Expected Family Contribution (EFC)
- Undergraduates only
- Lifetime limit: 12 semesters
- No repayment required

**Federal Supplemental Educational Opportunity Grant (FSEOG):**
- For students with exceptional need
- $100 - $4,000 per year
- Limited funding (awarded first-come, first-served)

**TEACH Grant:**
- For students planning to teach
- Up to $4,000 per year
- Requirements:
  - Teach in high-need field (math, science, special ed)
  - At low-income school
  - For 4 years within 8 years of graduation
- **Converts to loan** if don't fulfill teaching obligation

### State Grants

**State Grant Program:**
- Varies by state
- Typically need-based
- Must be state resident
- May require separate application
- Amounts: $500 - $5,000+ depending on state

**Check Eligibility:**
1. Complete FAFSA (most states use this)
2. Check state education department website
3. Some states have separate application

### Institutional Grants

**University Grant:**
- Awarded by our university
- Need-based
- Automatically considered when you apply for aid
- No separate application
- Amounts vary ($2,000 - $20,000+)

**Renewal:**
- File FAFSA each year
- Maintain satisfactory academic progress
- Grant amount may change based on financial need

## Student Loans

### Federal Direct Loans

**Subsidized Loans:**
- Need-based
- Government pays interest while in school
- Undergraduates only
- Annual limits:
  - Freshman: $3,500
  - Sophomore: $4,500
  - Junior/Senior: $5,500

**Unsubsidized Loans:**
- Not need-based (everyone eligible)
- Interest accrues while in school
- Can pay interest or let it capitalize
- Annual limits:
  - Dependent: $2,000 (in addition to subsidized)
  - Independent: $6,000-7,000 (in addition to subsidized)

**Aggregate Limits:**
- Dependent undergrads: $31,000 total
- Independent undergrads: $57,500 total

### Accepting Federal Loans

**Steps:**
1. Accept loan amount in award letter (can reduce)
2. **Complete Entrance Counseling** (first-time borrowers):
   - Navigate to `studentaid.gov`
   - Login with FSA ID
   - Complete online tutorial (~30 minutes)
   - Learn about rights and responsibilities
3. **Sign Master Promissory Note (MPN)**:
   - Legal document promising to repay
   - Valid for 10 years
   - Sign electronically at `studentaid.gov`
4. Loan funds disbursed to student account

**Interest Rates (2025-26 academic year):**
- Undergrad: ~4.99% (fixed)
- Graduate: ~6.54% (fixed)
- Parent PLUS: ~7.54% (fixed)
- Rates change annually on July 1

### Parent PLUS Loans

**What is it?**
- Federal loan for parents of dependent students
- Parent is borrower (responsible for repayment)
- Amount: Up to cost of attendance minus other aid

**Eligibility:**
- Parent cannot have adverse credit history
- Credit check required (soft inquiry)

**Application:**
1. Parent goes to `studentaid.gov`
2. Apply for PLUS loan
3. If approved, select amount
4. Complete PLUS MPN
5. Funds sent to student account

**Alternatives if Denied:**
- Parent can request reconsideration with endorser
- Student becomes eligible for additional $4,000-5,000 in unsubsidized loans

### Private Loans

**What are Private Loans?**
- Loans from banks, credit unions, other lenders
- Not federal (different terms and protections)
- Use only after exhausting federal options

**Considerations:**
- Variable or fixed interest rates (typically higher than federal)
- May require cosigner (parent or relative)
- Credit check required
- Fewer repayment options
- No income-driven repayment or forgiveness

**Applying:**
1. Compare lenders (rates, terms, fees)
2. Apply online with lender
3. Provide school information
4. Lender certifies amount with financial aid office
5. Funds disbursed if approved

## Work-Study

### Federal Work-Study (FWS)

**What is it?**
- Part-time jobs to earn money for education expenses
- Federal program
- Award amount: Typically $2,000 - $4,000 per year
- You earn award through hourly work (not automatic)

**Eligibility:**
- Must demonstrate financial need
- Awarded through FAFSA
- Limited funding (not all eligible students receive it)

### Finding Work-Study Jobs

**Job Board:**
1. Navigate to **Financial Aid** → **Work-Study Jobs**
2. Browse available positions:
   - **On-Campus**: Library, dining, admin offices, tutoring, IT
   - **Off-Campus**: Community service organizations (limited)
   - **Department-Specific**: Related to your major
3. Filter by:
   - Location
   - Schedule (hours per week)
   - Pay rate ($10-15/hour typically)
   - Department

**Application:**
1. Click **Apply** on job posting
2. Submit resume and cover letter (if required)
3. Employer reviews applications
4. Interview scheduled
5. If hired, complete hiring paperwork

**No Work-Study Award?**
- Still can apply for regular student jobs
- Check **Student Employment** office for non-work-study positions

### Working Your Award

**Work Schedule:**
- Typically 10-15 hours per week
- Flexible around class schedule
- Cannot exceed award amount

**Getting Paid:**
- Hourly wage (biweekly paycheck)
- Paid for hours worked (like regular job)
- Earnings **not** automatically applied to student account
- You decide how to use money (tuition, expenses, savings)

**Tracking Earnings:**
1. Navigate to **Work-Study** → **Earnings**
2. View:
   - Hours worked this pay period
   - Total earned this year
   - Remaining award balance
3. Receive alert when approaching award limit

## Emergency Financial Assistance

### Emergency Grant Program

**What it covers:**
- Unexpected financial hardship
- Not covered by other aid
- Examples:
  - Medical emergency
  - Family emergency requiring travel
  - Loss of job or housing
  - Emergency car repair (for commuters)
  - Technology (laptop) for remote learning

**Application:**
1. Navigate to **Financial Aid** → **Emergency Assistance**
2. Complete emergency grant application:
   - Describe emergency
   - Amount requested ($100 - $1,500 typically)
   - Supporting documentation (bills, receipts, estimates)
3. Submit application
4. Review by financial aid staff (typically 24-48 hours)
5. If approved, funds disbursed within 3-5 business days

**Important:**
- Grants are one-time (limited per student)
- Not guaranteed (based on availability and need)
- Must exhaust other options first

### Short-Term Emergency Loans

**What it is:**
- Small, short-term loan ($100 - $500)
- Must repay within 30-60 days
- No interest

**Use Cases:**
- Textbooks before financial aid disburses
- Rent payment while waiting for refund
- Emergency expense

**Application:**
1. Apply through financial aid office
2. Sign promissory note
3. Receive funds (same day or next day)
4. Repay by due date
5. Failure to repay: Hold on account (can't register or get transcript)

## Disbursement & Refunds

### When Aid is Disbursed

**Disbursement Schedule:**
- **Fall Semester**: Late August (before classes start)
- **Spring Semester**: Late December/Early January
- **Summer**: May

**Disbursement Process:**
1. Aid credited to student account
2. Tuition, fees, housing charges deducted
3. If aid exceeds charges, refund issued
4. If charges exceed aid, balance owed

**Requirements for Disbursement:**
- Be enrolled at least half-time (6 credits)
- Complete verification (if selected)
- Accept aid in financial aid portal
- Complete loan entrance counseling and MPN (for loans)

### Refunds

**What is a Refund?**
- Excess financial aid after all charges paid
- Used for books, supplies, living expenses

**Refund Timeline:**
- 14 days after disbursement (federal requirement)
- Typically 7-10 days at our university

**Refund Methods:**
1. **Direct Deposit** (recommended, fastest):
   - Set up in student account portal
   - Provide bank routing and account numbers
   - Refund deposited 3-5 business days
2. **Paper Check**:
   - Mailed to address on file
   - Takes 10-14 days

**Setting Up Direct Deposit:**
1. Navigate to **Student Account** → **Refund Preferences**
2. Enter bank information
3. Verify account (small test deposits)
4. Future refunds auto-deposited

### Return of Title IV Funds

**If You Withdraw:**
- Federal law requires returning unearned aid
- If withdraw before 60% of semester, must return portion of aid
- Can result in balance owed to university
- Must repay federal government for grants

**Calculation:**
- Percentage of semester completed = days attended / total days
- Unearned percentage returned to federal programs
- You may owe money

**Consequences:**
- Balance on account (can't register or get transcript)
- Must repay federal grants (may lose future aid eligibility)

## Financial Literacy

### Budgeting Tools

**Monthly Budget Calculator:**
1. Navigate to **Financial Aid** → **Financial Literacy**
2. Use budget calculator
3. Input:
   - Income (work-study, outside job, family support)
   - Fixed expenses (rent, utilities, phone)
   - Variable expenses (food, entertainment, transportation)
4. View report:
   - Income vs. expenses
   - Recommended adjustments
   - Savings goals

### Loan Counseling

**Entrance Counseling:**
- Required before receiving first federal loan
- Complete at `studentaid.gov`
- Topics:
  - How loans work
  - Interest rates and fees
  - Repayment options
  - Rights and responsibilities

**Exit Counseling:**
- Required before graduating or dropping below half-time
- Review repayment plans and options
- Understand deferment and forbearance
- Loan servicer contact information

### Loan Repayment Simulator

**Plan for Repayment:**
1. Visit `studentaid.gov/loan-simulator`
2. Enter your loans (amounts and types)
3. View repayment plans:
   - **Standard**: Fixed payment, 10 years
   - **Graduated**: Lower payments initially, increase over time
   - **Extended**: Lower payments, up to 25 years
   - **Income-Driven**: Based on income and family size, 20-25 years
4. Compare monthly payments and total interest
5. Choose plan when you enter repayment

## Best Practices

### Maximizing Aid

1. **File FAFSA Every Year**: Even if you don't think you qualify
2. **Meet Deadlines**: Priority deadline = more aid
3. **Apply for Scholarships**: Free money that doesn't need repayment
4. **Maintain GPA**: Keep scholarships and avoid probation
5. **Communicate**: Contact financial aid office if circumstances change

### Minimizing Debt

1. **Borrow Only What You Need**: Don't max out loans
2. **Exhaust Free Aid First**: Grants, scholarships before loans
3. **Work Part-Time**: Reduce loan need
4. **Live Affordably**: Budget and minimize expenses
5. **Graduate On Time**: Extra semesters = more cost

### Avoiding Common Mistakes

1. **Missing Deadlines**: Late FAFSA = less aid
2. **Not Reading Award Letter**: Understand what you're accepting
3. **Ignoring Email**: Financial aid office sends important notifications
4. **Dropping Below Half-Time**: Triggers loan repayment
5. **Not Updating Address**: Miss important mail
